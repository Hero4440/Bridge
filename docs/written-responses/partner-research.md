# Partner research and insights

We partnered with First Place AZ, a supported-living community that helps neurodivergent adults build independence. On our first visit, we learned that residents already have an events app, so we built Bridge to complement it rather than replace it. Josh Munoz told us connection is one of residents' biggest needs: without friends, "you're not going to want to do anything," and self-care and work both suffer. That shifted us away from a generic planning tool toward social connection.

Josh's feedback on our concept led to five changes:

1. **"Social connection looks different for everyone."** The daily check-in now asks what each person wants help with: meeting new people, staying close to people they know, handling tricky moments, or talking at work or school. Home and practice adapt to the answer.
2. **Support existing relationships, not only new ones.** We added "My people" and practice scenarios for catching up with a friend, fixing a misunderstanding, and asking a roommate or boss for help.
3. **Avoid masking and "two AIs talking to each other."** Mo's reply ideas stay hidden until the user asks for them. Every practice level has an "Or say it your own way" box, and Mo never sends a message for the user.
4. **Fade support as confidence grows.** Practice moves from choices to hints to the user's own words, and users can turn Mo's help down from "Lots" to "When I ask."
5. **Plan for safety offline.** We added a "Before you meet" checklist for events: meet somewhere public, tell a trusted person, plan how you'll get home, and know you can leave anytime.
