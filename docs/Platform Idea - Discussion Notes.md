# Platform Idea — Discussion Notes

**Discussion date:** October 30, 2026  
**Status:** Early concept discussion. The feedback and MVP below are recommendations, not adopted requirements or a final scope.

## User's idea

Create a host application containing multiple installable sub-apps. A ChatGPT-like assistant would be available through text and voice, with personal context shared across the ecosystem so the user would not have to repeat the same information in every app.

### Profiles and access

The original proposal includes two profile types:

- **User**
- **Guardian**

Under the original proposal, guardians would have all controls over a user's profile, and a user could have multiple guardians. This describes the initial idea only; it is not a resolved permissions or privacy policy.

### Initial built-in apps

- Communication support, described in the discussion as “ACC.” This may mean **AAC (augmentative and alternative communication)** and should be confirmed.
- Cognition games
- Skill building
- Connecting with friends

Each app would contribute activity context to the shared assistant. For example, a game could record what was played and how the interaction went. The intent is for the assistant to use context from across the platform to provide continuity and more relevant support.

### Reporting

The platform could produce daily or monthly reports for guardians. Reports might also be viewable by the user. The reporting cadence, contents, access rules, and sharing controls have not been decided.

### Longer-term ecosystem

A later marketplace could allow other people or organizations to contribute apps and customize them within a user's ecosystem. The marketplace is part of the long-term vision, not an agreed first-prototype requirement.

## Assistant feedback and recommendations

### What appears promising

The strongest value proposition is continuity: information can follow the user across activities so they do not need to repeatedly explain their needs, preferences, or recent experiences. Supporting both voice and text could also give users more ways to participate.

The overall platform and marketplace are a promising long-term vision, but they are likely too broad for the first challenge prototype.

### Autonomy, privacy, and interpretation

Adult autonomy is important for First Place users. Before the access model becomes a requirement, the team should clarify:

- What information remains private to the user
- What the user can choose to share
- Which controls belong to supporters or legally authorized guardians
- How permissions change for different users and circumstances
- How conflicting decisions from multiple guardians or supporters are handled

The original statement that guardians have all controls should therefore remain a proposal, not be presented as settled policy.

Activity data also requires careful wording. Observations such as what someone played, whether they completed an activity, or how often they interacted do not by themselves establish cognitive ability or wellbeing. Reports should clearly separate observed behavior from interpretations or suggestions.

### Challenge rubric assessment

The concept has a strong vision, but the working functionality and measurable impact are not yet defined. The reviewed challenge materials emphasize:

- A working MVP
- User-centered support for neurodivergent needs, including executive function
- Impact and inclusivity
- Functionality
- Collaboration
- Principled innovation
- Creativity and effective use of tools
- A clear pitch and potential for growth

To score well, the concept still needs a specific user journey, a demonstrable outcome, and a way to evaluate whether the prototype helped.

### Suggested smallest MVP

One recommendation was to demonstrate a single complete journey inside the host app—for example, preparing for and participating in a First Place event—plus a simple, permission-based summary for a supporter. The marketplace and broader collection of apps could remain in the future vision.

This was an assistant recommendation only. The user did not explicitly accept it as the final scope.

## Relationship to the existing Bridge idea

The existing Bridge documents describe an event journey of preview, practice, attend/reset, and reflect. They remain separate concept work and have not been replaced by this platform discussion.

If the suggested event-based MVP is explored, Bridge could be treated as one possible sub-app or demonstration journey within the larger platform. That relationship has not been decided.

The Bridge v3 notes also contain an unresolved tension: coaches set goals while users define success. This platform discussion does not resolve that question.

## Unresolved decisions

1. Is the communication app correctly described as AAC rather than ACC?
2. Who is the first intended user, and what is the single most important problem the first prototype should solve?
3. Is the first deliverable a broad platform demonstration or one complete journey within the platform?
4. What personal context may each sub-app collect, and what context may the assistant use?
5. What information is private, shared by default, or shared only with the user's permission?
6. What does “guardian” mean in this product: a legal guardian, parent, coach, supporter, or another role?
7. When may a guardian or supporter override a user's choices, if ever?
8. How are permissions and disagreements handled when more than one guardian or supporter is connected?
9. What belongs in a report, who can view it, and should reports be daily, monthly, event-based, or user-triggered?
10. How will reports distinguish direct observations from interpretations?
11. What working functionality will be shown in the challenge prototype?
12. What measurable outcome will demonstrate usefulness or impact?
13. How, if at all, should the platform concept relate to Bridge?
14. Which marketplace capabilities belong only in the future vision?
